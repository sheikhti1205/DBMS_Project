\section{Source Discovery and Data Profile}
\label{sec:data}

Environmental data for Bangladesh is spread across government publications,
research files and international data platforms. The first task was therefore
not simply to collect as many files as possible, but to determine which files
contained records that could be represented by the approved model. The audited
source-collection snapshot contains \mAllDriveFiles{} files. Of these,
\mSourceDataFiles{} are collected source or data files, \mSupportFiles{} are
administrative or supporting files and \mDerivedArtifacts{} are formatted or
page-level derivatives created during review. These groups are reported
separately so that a generated page image is not counted as another publication.

\subsection{Discovery Overview}
\label{subsec:source-discovery}

The search covered official Bangladeshi organisations and international
platforms rather than relying on one repository. Each candidate was reviewed by
publisher, subject, record grain, time range, file type, missing-value notation
and the availability of a dependable key. Search engines and LLM tools helped
locate candidate pages, but acceptance depended on the actual publisher, file
contents and traceable source evidence.

The review reduced the wider collection to \mSourceFiles{} structured files
used by the loader. The reduction is not hidden as data loss: excluded files
remain in the source register with their reasons. The BBS workbook alone has
\mSheetsTotal{} sheets; \mSheetsUsed{} contribute implemented data and
\mSheetsExcluded{} have recorded exclusion decisions.

\subsection{Source Organisations and Selection}
\label{subsec:source-organisations}

Four organisations supplied the implemented records. BBS contributes climate,
water-quality, forest and industrial statistics. BMD supplies temperature data
and a sunshine publication; its temperature records contribute to the final
database, while the two BMD sunshine blocks cannot be loaded because the source
provides no usable station identity. BRRI supplies daily temperature,
rainfall, humidity and sunshine observations. BWDB supplies the river reference
used to connect monitoring stations with river names.

The project also reviewed files from the Department of Environment, the
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, HDX, the UN, UNESCO, WHO,
the World Bank and other publishers. Those files remain part of the review even
when they are not loaded. Common reasons include missing join identifiers,
incompatible grain, duplication of retained material, a subject outside the
approved model or narrative content without repeated observations.

\subsection{Environmental Data Categories}
\label{subsec:data-categories}

The integrated database contains environmental subjects supported by the
approved final ERD and the implemented SQLite relations. Collected subjects
without an implemented relation are not presented as database categories.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Climate and atmosphere:} Maximum and minimum temperature,
  rainfall in millimetres, relative humidity, wind speed and direction,
  sunshine, radiation, thunderstorm and lightning observations.
  \item \textbf{Rivers and water quality:} River and monitoring-station names
  together with pH, dissolved oxygen, BOD, COD, salinity and plastic or
  marine-debris parameter values.
  \item \textbf{Forest area:} Protected, reserved, unclassed, acquired and
  total forest area by district and fiscal period.
  \item \textbf{Industry:} Establishment size or industry category with
  quantity, percentage and fiscal period.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Source Selection and Exclusion}
\label{subsec:source-selection-exclusion}

Every registered candidate was inspected rather than accepted from its title or
metadata alone. The review considered Bangladesh and environmental relevance,
publisher traceability, usable record structure, observation coverage,
identifiers, duplication and fit with the approved ERD. Relevant narrative
publications remain supporting evidence even when they do not supply repeated
records for the database.

\subsection{Data Range and Record Grain}
\label{subsec:data-range}

The database covers \mYearMin--\mYearMax{} as a union of the implemented
relations, not as a promise that every subject is available in every year.
Monthly temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind and event records use
\texttt{(Station\_Name, Year, Month)} with an additional type where required.
Sunshine and radiation use day grain. Water quality uses station, year and
parameter while forest and industrial tables use fiscal spans.

The record grain determines whether two similarly named datasets can be related.
Daily station observations cannot be joined directly with annual district

remain null unless a retained source establishes the relationship.

\subsection{Source Discovery Challenges}
\label{subsec:source-discovery-challenges}

The most persistent discovery problem was locating usable official data.
Government websites often used inconsistent navigation, slow pages, broken
links or separate pages for related publications. The team therefore revisited
source pages and recorded the final publisher location rather than relying on a
search-result description.

The collected material also used different formats and structures. Excel files
contained merged cells, repeated headers, many worksheets and inconsistent
layouts. PDF reports sometimes placed relevant tables inside narrative pages,
while CSV files used their own names, date formats and missing-value notation.
Each format required inspection before the team could decide whether it
contained repeated, identifiable observations.

Similar variables were represented with different headings, dates, station
names and codes. These differences affected join potential and had to be
identified before modelling. Temporal coverage was also uneven: some variables
covered decades, while others described only short fiscal or observation
periods. The team retained these limits instead of implying uniform coverage.
